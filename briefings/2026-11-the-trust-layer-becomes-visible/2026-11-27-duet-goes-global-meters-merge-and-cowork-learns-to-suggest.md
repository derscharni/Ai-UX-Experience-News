## Product Highlights

### Claude / Anthropic: Duet Goes Global and the Tasks Panel Does the Transparency Work

Anthropic's most consequential interaction-design event in this 48-hour window is **Claude Duet**'s geographic expansion — the moment when a proactive ambient agent that has been a US-only research preview since October 21 opens its waitlist to four major new markets simultaneously.



Anthropic expanded the research preview of Claude Duet to India, with waitlist access for Pro, Max, and Ultra users, a public beta for Team plans, and a private beta for enterprises.

Anthropic's Claude Duet product page now includes India in its research-preview waitlist for Pro, Max and Ultra subscribers, and also lists Indonesia, Brazil and Japan alongside the US.

 The significance is not purely geographic. 

Anthropic opened a research preview of Claude Duet on October 21, 2026, a standalone app where Claude works as a proactive agent instead of waiting for the user to open a chat; in Anthropic's words, it "comes to you with what needs your attention, completes tasks you ask of it, keeps working when you step away, and checks with you before it acts on your behalf."



The UX architecture of Duet is what makes this expansion meaningful. 

The app has four spaces: Tasks shows live what Duet is working on and what needs user input, and Make holds finished work like briefs and decks.

Built around Opus 5.5, Duet carries over Claude memory, skills and connections; it can prepare forecasts, build web apps, and handle admin, but shows messages, payments, signatures, and deletions for approval first.

 The Tasks space is the trust-design element that most directly governs user confidence in a proactive agent: unlike a background agent that simply completes work and surfaces a result, a live task panel that distinguishes "in progress" from "needs your input" gives the user a continuous read on the agent's authority boundary — which steps it has resolved autonomously and which it has halted for human review. 

Duet can keep working in the cloud after the user closes the app, and it can coordinate with Claude Code sessions.



The access architecture of the expansion is itself a trust-design signal worth reading carefully. 

This is a waitlist expansion, not general availability.

Joining does not guarantee an invitation or a date, and documentation remains inconsistent: the product page now includes India but says Team and Enterprise are not included, while the getting-started help page still says US-only.

 This documentation gap — between what launch announcements described for Team and Enterprise access and what the product page reflects — is itself the kind of trust-design friction that the broader category is working to resolve: when the agent's access tier is ambiguous at the boundary, users cannot reliably know what they have been granted.

### ChatGPT / OpenAI: Dots Reach the Mid-Market, the Meter Merges, and GPT-6.2 Enters Chat

OpenAI's most consequential interaction-design event settling into broad availability in this window is **Dots for ChatGPT Business Standard** — the expansion that for the first time makes always-on proactive agents available below the premium Pro tier.



OpenAI is testing its always-on Dots agents with a small number of ChatGPT Plus subscribers and simultaneously rolling the feature out to all ChatGPT Business Standard users in the United States starting November 19; until now, Dots had been limited to Pro, Business Premium, and Enterprise plans.

Dots for ChatGPT Business Standard let eligible members create one personal dot in their Business workspace and ask it to help with ongoing projects, like preparing a weekly update; usage limits apply.

 The UX significance of this tier expansion is structural: an always-on agent that previously required a Pro or Business Premium subscription — and whose per-task usage didn't count toward regular limits for those plans — is now available to Business Standard members who pay significantly less. 

For higher-tier plans, conversations with the agents don't count toward regular ChatGPT usage limits for Pro and Business Premium subscribers, as long as they don't involve complex tasks; it's not clear if Plus or Business Standard users will get that benefit as well.

 That usage-limit ambiguity at the lower tiers is the trust-design detail that most requires clarification: when users cannot predict which of their Dots interactions will draw from their shared allowance, the proactive agent's economic model becomes opaque at exactly the moment adoption is widest.

The **unified Chat-and-Work usage meter** completing its Pro subscriber rollout is the resource-governance advance that changes how the heaviest ChatGPT users experience their subscription. 

An OpenAI spokesperson told TechCrunch that while most users are not expected to face more frequent usage resets, a small fraction of its heaviest users may hit their limits sooner; under the new system, Chat and Work will share a single usage meter across ChatGPT.

The rollout has started for a limited number of users, with OpenAI planning to bring shared usage limits more broadly in December.

"This will enable you to move from exploring an idea to having ChatGPT carry it through, all in one place," the company's earlier email to users had said; "Codex will remain a separate experience in the ChatGPT desktop app."

 The Codex carve-out is itself a trust-design signal: by keeping the developer coding-agent surface separate, OpenAI is distinguishing between conversational-to-agentic continuity (which the merge enables) and deep-code execution (which remains its own distinct workflow with its own compute accounting).



GPT-6.2 starts rolling out globally to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise tiers today in the Chat tab, with Enterprise availability depending on workplace admin settings.

This update applies to the Chat experience in ChatGPT; the models powering Work and Codex are not changing as part of this release.

 This Chat-only scope is consistent with the unified-meter architecture: a Chat model upgrade that deliberately does not change Work or Codex preserves the resource-accounting boundary that separates everyday conversational use from agentic execution.

### Google Gemini: Camera Becomes Conversational and GIFs Join the Prompt

Google's most consequential interaction-design advance settling into broad availability in this window is the **Gemini Android camera redesign** — the transition from a full-screen capture interruption to an inline bottom-sheet that opens directly within the conversation or the Gemini homepage.



Google has rolled out a major inline camera redesign for the Gemini app on Android, replacing the older full-screen viewfinder with a fluid bottom-sheet interface; the updated layout appears directly on the Gemini homepage or inside active conversations, and users can drag the bottom sheet upward to fill most of the screen.

This camera redesign first launched in the Gemini for iOS app, but has now widely rolled out on Android in both the beta and stable (version 17.76+) channels.

By keeping the camera inline rather than jumping to an isolated screen, the application maintains conversational continuity.

 This is the multimodal-interaction design decision that most directly reduces the cognitive cost of visual input: a user who can capture an image without leaving a conversation and can immediately see it as part of their prompt retains the thread of their intent in a way that a full-screen viewfinder interruption breaks. The old design required a mode switch; the new one makes visual input a natural gesture within an ongoing exchange.

The **GIF creation** feature launching alongside the camera redesign adds a generative output modality to Gemini's prompt response surface. 

The Gemini app can now create animated GIFs from a short text prompt, photos, or a video clip; Google announced the feature on the Gemini app's X account on November 20, and it runs on Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash.

Each GIF loops for about three seconds and can be copied straight into a chat, an email, or a doc.

GIF creation is a separate feature from the Android camera redesign; users deciding between the two should use the camera for image capture and the GIF option when they want a generated animation.

 The UX significance of the Format menu placement is the discovery-design decision that most shapes how widely this capability is adopted: a generative output format that lives in a dropdown alongside text, Markdown, and code is one that users encounter in the natural flow of composing a response rather than as a separate tool they must navigate to. The separation from the camera interface also establishes a clear conceptual model: capture is for input, GIF generation is for output.

### Microsoft Copilot: Chat Learns to Route Itself, Cowork Gets a Skill Console

Microsoft's most consequential interaction-design event completing its rollout in this window is **Copilot Chat's mode-switching suggestion** — the first production deployment of a Copilot surface that proactively identifies when the user's intent belongs in a different execution environment and offers to move them there.



Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat will suggest switching to a better-fit Copilot mode — such as Cowork — after a prompt, maintaining conversation context; this feature rolls out worldwide mid to late November 2026 and affects users who have access to supported modes.

Users will see a suggestion card after they send a prompt in Chat, with an explanation of the better-fit mode and an option for the user to switch to the suggested mode; their chat conversation will be attached to the prompt in the new mode to maintain context.

 The UX shift here is from a user-navigated multi-surface product to a self-routing one: previously, a user who typed a complex multi-step task into Copilot Chat would receive a Chat-style answer; now, Copilot recognises that the task fits an agentic execution surface and surfaces the routing suggestion itself, with the full conversation carried forward. 

Users will see suggestions only for experiences they can access

 — which is itself the trust-design constraint that makes the routing legible: the system only recommends surfaces the user can actually use, eliminating suggestion cards that lead to access-denied screens.

The **Cowork Plugins page** renaming from "Customize" is the workspace-organisation advance of the Microsoft window. 

The Plugins page now hosts plugins and skills; users can browse, install, and share plugins and skills in one place, and create new skills with a guided conversation.

 The consolidation of two previously separate configuration surfaces into one is the navigation-design decision that most reduces the administrative cost of Cowork customisation: when skills and plugins live in the same location with a guided creation flow, the action of extending the agent's capability is as discoverable as browsing its existing tools. 

Proactive recommendations powered by Work IQ read the signals across email, meetings, and Teams chats and suggest the next task worth giving to Cowork, like preparing for tomorrow's customer call or closing out a thread that's waiting on the user; because Work IQ understands the user's work, relationships, and organisation, those suggestions reflect what actually matters that week.

### Grok (xAI): Build 1.0.92 Closes the Gaps in Headless Agent Observability

xAI's interaction-design story in this window is defined by **Grok Build 1.0.92** — the November 19 release that most directly addresses the class of observability and state-continuity failures that erode trust in headless agentic coding workflows.



Grok Build 1.0.92 improves headless image output, local file links, question cards, session resume guidance, and hook inspection.

 The specific fixes merit individual UX analysis. 

MCP tool results that contain images now include those images in headless streaming and JSON output; session resume errors now give the same helpful instruction on every route instead of suggesting the TUI picker.

 The image-in-headless-output fix is the agentic-output fidelity advance: when an MCP tool produces an image as part of its result and that image was previously stripped from headless streaming, the agent's output summary was incomplete — a developer reading the JSON representation of a task could miss visual results that the agent had actually generated. The session-resume consistency fix addresses a different class of failure: 

question cards in minimal mode now keep the first option visible instead of scrolling it off-screen

, eliminating an interface state where the user could not see the choices available to them at a decision point without scrolling — the equivalent of presenting a confirmation dialog with the buttons pushed below the visible area.



Grok Build improves headless image output, local file links, question cards, session resume guidance, and hook inspection in its 1.0.92 release.

 The hook-inspection improvement — 

grok inspect now shows the hooks a headless run would actually load, with a note for those that still need folder trust

 — is the trust-transparency advance that most directly governs how developers reason about an automated agent's configuration before they deploy it: an inspect command that shows the actual runtime hook set, including which hooks are blocked by trust requirements, gives the developer a pre-flight view of the agent's permission state rather than discovering gaps when execution fails. 

Grok 4.8 is not generally available as of November 24, 2026; Musk wrote on November 17 that it has had only trials behind the scenes, and on November 19 that it has several model variants in beta with no pick yet for wide release.

### Perplexity: The November 24 Wave Settles In, and the Model Selector Gains Five Frontiers

Perplexity's most significant interaction-design story in this window is the continued global settling of its November 24 changelog wave, now confirmed reaching Mac in addition to iOS and Android, alongside the expansion of its **frontier model selector** in search to five new options including GPT-6.2 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2.



Multiagent, which runs a team of agents across different models, reached iOS, Android, and Mac for Max and Enterprise Max subscribers, and search gained five selectable frontier models including GPT-6.2 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2.

 The model-selector expansion is the agent-transparency UX advance of the Perplexity window: in a platform that has historically offered a "Best" automatic selection, adding explicit frontier model choices — with named identifiers — transforms the search interaction from an opaque process into an auditable one where the user can verify which reasoning system produced a given answer, reproduce the query under the same conditions, and compare results across models for high-stakes research. 

Perplexity's Multiagent mode splits a big task across up to 16 AI agents that check each other's work and return one result, and arrived on November 24 for Perplexity Max and Enterprise Max subscribers on web, iOS, Android, and Mac.



Time Machine's production significance continues settling into enterprise operator awareness this window. 

Perplexity CEO Aravind Srinivas announced the feature on X, describing it as a way to help AI agents learn from historical information and improve their ability to predict outcomes.

An AI forecasting agent can examine archived news reports, public information, and other webpages available before a particular event, then generate a prediction based on that material; developers can subsequently compare the prediction with the actual outcome to measure the model's performance; according to Perplexity, the model's score increased from 0.77 to 0.80 after training with Time Machine.

 The score improvement is the trust-design signal that moves Time Machine from a developer-infrastructure curiosity to a production evaluation tool: a measurable improvement in forecasting performance from removing hindsight contamination is the empirical case that point-in-time retrieval is not just an audit feature but an accuracy feature — the agent that cannot look up the answer is the agent that must actually reason toward it, and that reasoning is what Time Machine makes measurable.

## The Bigger Picture: Duet Goes Global, Meters Merge, and Cowork Learns to Suggest

The 48 hours ending November 27, 2026 crystallise a single macro movement that the final week of November has been building toward: **the AI agent category is completing the transition from capability-first expansion to access-layer renegotiation — and every significant interaction-design event in this window is an answer to the same underlying question: who gets the proactive agent, what does it cost them, and how does the interface communicate its own authority boundaries clearly enough to be trusted?** Claude Duet reaching India, Indonesia, Brazil, and Japan is the clearest geographic expression: a proactive ambient agent that previously required US presence and a specific subscription tier is now a waitlist product across four of the world's largest digital-consumer markets, with a four-space interface — Tasks, Chat, Make, Channels — that makes the agent's live work and its approval checkpoints simultaneously visible. Dots landing on ChatGPT Business Standard answers the same access question at the subscription layer: an always-on proactive agent that was previously a premium differentiator is now part of the mid-market enterprise baseline, and the question of whether Business Standard usage counts against the shared meter is the unresolved trust-design edge that will shape how aggressively those users delegate. The unified Chat-and-Work meter answers the cost question by collapsing separate allowances into one legible budget — but it simultaneously introduces a constraint that disproportionately affects the heaviest agentic users, the exact users who most depend on proactive agents to manage their workload. Copilot's mode-suggestion banner answers the routing question: when Chat identifies that a prompt belongs in Cowork and offers to carry the conversation there with context intact, it is the first production deployment of an interface that actively manages its own authority boundaries — routing the user toward the agent tier that can most fully execute their intent rather than handling the task at a lower capability level and returning an incomplete result. And Grok Build 1.0.92's question-card, hook-inspection, and session-resume improvements answer the observability question at the developer layer: the developer who can see exactly which hooks a headless agent will load, receive consistent resume guidance at every failure path, and trust that image outputs survive JSON serialisation is the developer who can delegate agentic execution without manually auditing the agent's configuration before each run. What connects all five — Duet's Tasks panel, Dots' tier expansion, the unified meter, Copilot's routing card, and Build's pre-flight inspect — is the same design conclusion the category has been arriving at throughout November 2026: the platforms that earn the deepest delegation are the ones that make their authority architecture visible and legible at every access layer, not the ones that simply add capabilities and assume users will figure out what the agent is doing on their behalf.
